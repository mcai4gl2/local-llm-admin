## Splash continuation preparation and regression fixes

### Problem/Goal

Complete the remaining counterbalanced timing sessions for stock 27B 8-bit and modified
27B 4-bit without exceeding a live maintenance bound or treating partial/native diagnostics
as an adoption pass. Required regression checks also exposed a download-test timing assumption
and a pre-existing log-maintenance submission/completion race.

### Solution/Implementation

- Added a test-first, offline continuation planner. It validates complete paired histories,
  frozen conditions, contiguous alternating sessions, token accounting and all supplied failures.
  It budgets the slowest recorded request per case, including warmups, with an explicit slowdown
  margin and setup/switch/restoration reserves. Whole pairs must fit; private output is exclusive.
  It never contacts a server or performs a lifecycle operation.
- Prepared two separate proposed windows, capped at two hours each, with restoration and
  verification between them. Session 2 is Splash then MLX; session 3 is MLX then Splash for
  each selected checkpoint. Each budgets 107.20 minutes, including a 25% request slowdown margin,
  15 minutes for setup/switches and 10 minutes for restoration. New live approval is pending.
- Audited the actual pinned timing sources and published six verified fingerprints. MLX ends
  its rate interval at last emission and reconstructs predicted duration from its rate; Splash
  uses first-emission-to-Done timing and excludes the first native batch. Equivalence remains
  unproven. Preserve the original wall metric and session-1 evidence; additional sessions will
  not independently pass the native decode or full adoption gates.
- Captured a private read-only inventory: semantic restore state unchanged, no active admin jobs,
  and no experiment listener/processes. Fresh identity/readiness and asset review remain required
  before any future activation. No live service was stopped or deployed in this continuation.
- Fixed the downloader test's running-state assumption: after proving the second download is
  rejected as busy, verify that the first successfully publishes instead of requiring it still
  to be running. The original assertion independently failed when it observed publishing.
- Reproduced the maintenance race deterministically with three initially failing success,
  deferral and failure callback-order tests. Serialize maintenance state read/modify/write
  operations and preserve the same job's completion fields when submission metadata arrives
  later. Use fresh state on refused submissions. Hold no new lock across an await, subprocess,
  job submission or alert update. Daily/retry policy, inference and host wrappers are unchanged.
